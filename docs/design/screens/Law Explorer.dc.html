⌕ Who can end a state of emergency in California? EN · ES · 中文 · TL · VI
Can the governor suspend laws? What happens if the governor dies? Who counts the votes? Can protests be banned?
ANSWERED FROM CORPUS V41 · 4 CITATIONS · CONFIDENCE C2

Two ways. The governor can declare it over at any time [§8629]. Or the Legislature can end it by passing a concurrent resolution — a majority vote of both houses, no governor's signature needed [§8629(a)].

What the law doesn't have: any automatic end. If nobody acts, the emergency continues indefinitely [§8627] — that's finding VLN-00142, and there's a proposed fix (PATCH-0091).

Termination of a state of emergency proclaimed under Gov. Code §8625 occurs by (1) gubernatorial proclamation under §8629, or (2) concurrent resolution of the Legislature per §8629(a) — a non-delegable check requiring no executive assent. No statutory sunset attaches to the underlying declaration or to §8627 delegated powers; cf. Newsom v. Superior Court (2021) 63 Cal.App.5th 1099 on the scope of §8627 during extended emergencies. Automatic-termination provisions exist in 24 other states (see Compare).
