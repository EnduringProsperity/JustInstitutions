Emergency powers get a clock

PATCH-0091 · from finding VLN-00142 · /us/ca/patch/0091

Today, a California emergency lasts until someone chooses to end it. This patch adds what the law forgot: a 60-day sunset on delegated emergency powers, renewable only by a legislature that has to show up and vote.

The finding: Gov. Code §8627 grants the governor plenary authority over state agencies during a declared emergency with no time limit — a partial verdict on test A5 Delegation Limits.

Feasibility — what kind of change is this?

A statute amendment to the Emergency Services Act — no constitutional change required. Majority vote in both chambers plus the governor's signature… which is the political catch, since the patch limits the signer.

difficulty 3/5 · typical timeline: one to two legislative sessions
2

Precedent — who has already fixed this?

Kentucky
SB 1 (2021) · statute

Emergency orders expire at 30 days unless the legislature extends. Survived a court challenge in 2022.

Pennsylvania
Const. amend. (2021) · ballot

Voters capped disaster declarations at 21 days; extension requires a concurrent resolution.

Minnesota
Ch. 12 §12.31 · statute

Peacetime emergencies past 30 days require legislative consent; the legislature can end one by majority vote.

Beneficiaries — who gains from the bug, who pays?

GAINS FROM THE CURRENT STATE

Any sitting executive, of either party — open-ended authority is convenient. Agencies operating under emergency procurement rules. Vendors holding no-bid emergency contracts.

PAYS THE COST

The legislature's institutional authority — and the public, diffusely: oversight of emergency spending weakens the longer a declaration runs. Diffuse cost is why the bug persists.

Coalition map: legislators of both parties when out of the governor's party; civil-liberties and government-transparency groups; county governments. Opposition: the executive branch, emergency-management associations warning about mid-crisis lapses — the 60-day term with unlimited renewals is what answers them.

4

Draft language

AI-DRAFTED — REQUIRES EXPERT LEGAL REVIEW BEFORE ANY USE refactoring: Introduce Sunset Clause

Section 8627.5 is added to the Government Code, to read:

(a) Any authority delegated to the Governor pursuant to Section 8627 shall expire sixty (60) days after the proclamation of the state of emergency, unless extended by concurrent resolution of the Legislature for successive periods not to exceed sixty (60) days each.

(b) Upon expiration pursuant to subdivision (a), any order or regulation issued under the delegated authority shall cease to have effect, except as necessary for orderly wind-down not exceeding fourteen (14) days.

Scope: changes only the duration of delegated §8627 authority. Does not touch the declaration itself (§8625), mutual-aid, or funding provisions — §8645's appropriation gap is a separate finding (VLN-00214).

Unintended consequences — what could this break?

The patch is applied to a copy of the corpus and re-run: every section that references the changed authority is scanned for conflicts, and the incentive it creates is analyzed against how peers gamed the same fix.

CONFLICT

§8571 suspensions and mutual-aid agreements draw on §8627 authority — an expiry mid-incident could void active orders. Mitigation: conforming amendments + the 14-day wind-down in subdivision (b).

addressed in draft
INCENTIVE

Serial re-declaration: end the emergency, re-proclaim the same event, reset the clock — the workaround peers saw after similar sunsets. Mitigation: successive proclamations for one underlying event count against a single clock.

needs draft change
CAPACITY

A long emergency with the legislature out of session could lapse powers mid-crisis. Mitigation: unlimited 60-day renewals plus a special-session trigger on pending expiry.

needs draft change
DRIFT

Renewal votes could become pro forma — a rubber stamp restores the status quo in practice. No drafting fix; tracked as a renewal-margin KPI after passage (step 5).

Pathway — how it actually passes

Assembly bill → Governmental Organization committees → floor votes → governor. Key veto point: the governor's desk. If vetoed, the fallback is the Pennsylvania route — a legislatively-referred constitutional amendment bypasses the signature entirely.

Minimum viable patch: a report-and-review requirement (difficulty 2) — every 60 days the administration must justify continuation in a public hearing. No sunset, most of the oversight.
